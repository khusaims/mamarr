## Where it is and why it matters

Sohar sits on the Al Batinah coast, about 200 km north-west of Muscat and close to the UAE border. Its position on the Gulf of Oman means ships reach it **without transiting the Strait of Hormuz** — an advantage that becomes tangible every time tension in the strait rises or war-risk premiums for the passage go up.

## What it handles

The port is as much an industrial complex as a commercial harbour: a container terminal, berths for dry and liquid bulk, vehicle and equipment handling (Ro-Ro), and a refinery, petrochemical complex and aluminium and steel plants fed directly from its quays. Sohar Freezone is adjacent, allowing goods to be stored, processed or re-exported without entering the local market and paying its duties.

## What an importer needs to know

- **The natural destination** for cargo bound for Muscat, Al Batinah and the interior by road, and for re-export into the UAE.
- **Direct services** from Asia call regularly, but a good share of cargo still transships via Jebel Ali or Salalah; ask your forwarder whether the service is direct or relayed — the difference is days and money.
- **Free time and storage** are governed by the port tariff and the shipping line's terms together — know both before the container arrives.
- **Clearance** runs through Bayan, with customs present inside the port.

## Who typically uses it

Industrial importers in Al Batinah, building-material and equipment traders in the north of the country, and exporters from the industrial complex. As road and proposed rail links towards the UAE expand, its role as a land–sea alternative to Jebel Ali keeps growing.
