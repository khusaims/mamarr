## Where it is and why it matters

Salalah is in the far south of Oman, in Dhofar governorate, on the Arabian Sea coast. What sets it apart is not the size of its local market but its position: mega-vessels moving between Asia and Europe, and services from East Africa, deviate very little from their course to call there, which is why it became one of the region's most important transshipment ports. When the Red Sea or Hormuz is disrupted, Salalah becomes the relay point through which a great deal of Gulf-bound cargo flows.

## What it handles

A large container terminal run to global standards, through which the great majority of boxes are relayed to Gulf, Red Sea, East African and Indian subcontinent ports, alongside Oman's own import and export containers. A general cargo and bulk terminal serves the factories of Salalah Free Zone, whose exports include building materials, agricultural produce and processed goods.

## What an importer needs to know

- **If your cargo is bound for Sohar or Jebel Ali**, there is a fair chance it will pass through Salalah on a feeder vessel; that adds days but may be cheaper than a direct service.
- **The natural gateway** for importers in Dhofar and its surroundings, and for trade with Yemen and East Africa.
- **Salalah Free Zone** allows storage, processing and re-export with broad exemptions.
- **The monsoon season (khareef)** from June to September occasionally affects operations; account for it in time-critical schedules.

## Who typically uses it

Global shipping lines as a transshipment call, Dhofar and free-zone exporters, importers in the south, and traders working with Yemen and the Horn of Africa.
